# ESEF / IFRS-DK notes for a reader/parser (not a preparer tool)

Sources read in full:
- ESEF Reporting Manual, ESMA32-60-254 Rev, October 2025 update (`esef_reporting_manual_2025.txt`, 2767 lines) — cited below as "Guidance X.Y.Z"
- ESEF XBRL Taxonomy 2024 Documentation, 8 January 2025 (`esef_taxonomy_2024_documentation.txt`, 420 lines) — cited as "Taxonomy Doc §X"
- ESEF validation rules summary converted from an xlsx (`esef_validation_rules_2022.txt`, 4797 lines, essentially the ESEF-specific and IFRS-formula-linkbase-derived assertion definitions) — cited as "Validation rules: <rule id>"

Everything below is restricted to what actually matters for *consuming* an ESEF/IFRS inline-XBRL instance programmatically, not for producing one.

---

## 1. Which statements must be tagged; consolidated vs. separate/parent statements

**Tagging obligation is triggered by consolidated IFRS statements, not by IFRS reporting per se.**

- Guidance 4.1.1 is explicit: *"Article 4 paragraph 1 requires that issuers mark up their annual financial reports only when those include IFRS consolidated financial statements. Therefore issuers preparing only non-consolidated financial statements (in IFRS or in national GAAP) are not subject to the obligation to tag their financial statements as per Annex II paragraph 2 ... since tagging needs to be applied only by issuers preparing consolidated IFRS Financial statements."*
- Consequence for a parser: **if a filer has no subsidiaries and only prepares separate/parent financial statements, there may be no XBRL/iXBRL tagging at all** — only a plain XHTML report. A CVR-API-style parser must detect "no inline XBRL facts present" as a valid, expected state for such filers, not an error.
- Guidance 4.1.2 (Investment Entities exempted from consolidation): *"Investment entities that fall under the consolidation exception as per IFRS 10 ... and that only prepare individual IFRS financial statements are therefore not required to tag their IFRS financial statements."* Same consequence as above.
- "Primary Financial Statements" (defined in the Glossary) = statement of financial position, statement(s) of profit or loss and OCI, statement of changes in equity, statement of cash flows. These are the ones subject to Annex II mandatory tagging (Guidance 1.3.3, 1.9.1) when consolidated IFRS statements are present. Notes are subject only to *block tagging* with Annex II text-block elements where applicable (Guidance 1.9.1–1.9.3), not to full Annex II line-item tagging.

**When both consolidated and separate/parent statements appear in the same AFR and both get tagged (voluntarily, for the separate ones — see below), they are distinguished by a dimension, not by separate instances.** The manual references (only by label, in illustrative XBRL structures, not by literal QName) an axis/member pair:

- *"Consolidated and separate financial statements [axis]"* with default member *"Consolidated [member]"* (shown in the example structures under Guidance 3.4.2 and 3.4.3, and again in Taxonomy Doc §3.4.5: *"‘Line items not dimensionally qualified’ hypercube linking to ‘Consolidated (member)’ of ‘Consolidated and separate financial statement (axis)’ dimension."*).
- **The manual never gives the literal QName** for this axis/member (e.g. it is commonly known from the IFRS Taxonomy itself as `ifrs-full:ConsolidatedAndSeparateFinancialStatementsAxis` / `ifrs-full:ConsolidatedMember` / `ifrs-full:SeparateMember`, but that QName is **not verified in these three documents** — treat it as needing confirmation against the actual ESEF/IFRS taxonomy schema files before hard-coding it).
- Because `Consolidated [member]` is the **default member** of this axis (per the glossary's "domain" definition: *"When a fact does not have any domain member specified, that means it applies to the entire domain or to a default member of a domain set in the taxonomy"*), **a fact with no explicit dimensional qualification on this axis is implicitly a consolidated fact.** A parser that ignores dimensions entirely and just reads every `Assets`/`Equity`/etc. fact in the default (non-dimensional) context is, by construction, reading consolidated figures — which is exactly what's wanted for group financials. If the AFR also voluntarily tags separate/parent-only figures, those would appear as *dimensionally-qualified* facts (segment/scenario axis = e.g. "Separate [member]") and should be excluded from a "consolidated" read.

**Nothing in the three documents states that separate/parent statements are "prohibited" from being tagged** — only that they are *not mandatory*. Where an issuer chooses to voluntarily tag them (e.g. to have one coherent iXBRL document covering both), the axis above is the documented mechanism. There's no other RTS-Annex-II specific carve-out described for separate statements in these documents.

## 2. Context rules

- **Entity identifier / LEI** (Guidance 2.1.1): the `xbrli:identifier` **must** contain a valid LEI (ISO 17442), and its `scheme` attribute must be exactly `"http://standards.iso.org/iso/17442"`. Example given verbatim:
  ```xml
  <xbrli:entity>
    <xbrli:identifier scheme="http://standards.iso.org/iso/17442">KGCEPHLVVKVRZYO1T647</xbrli:identifier>
  </xbrli:entity>
  ```
  The ESEF/LEI taxonomy embeds checksum/format validation (`lei-identifier-checksum`, `lei-identifier-format`, `lei-identifier-scheme` — Validation rules, "LEI validations" group).
- **Single issuer per document** (Guidance 2.1.4 / rule `con_IdentifierValueMustBeIdentical`): *"All entity identifiers and schemes in contexts MUST have identical content."* A parser can assume exactly one LEI value across the whole instance; if it sees more than one, the file is non-conformant (or the parser mis-scoped the document set).
- **Period formatting** (Guidance 2.1.2): dates should be plain `yyyy-mm-dd` with **no time component and no time zone** (rule `con_ReportingPeriodMustUseYYYYMMDDFormatInstant`/`...DurationStart`/`...DurationEnd` check `not(matches(string($x),'T'))`).
- **Instant-date-off-by-one pitfall** (Guidance 2.1.2, easy to miss): *"the XBRL specification requires that preparers creating XBRL contexts for elements defined with period type instant in their ESEF submissions shall include the date 202(X-1)-12-31 instead of 202(X)-01-01 in xbrli:instant element of such context."* I.e. an opening balance-sheet context for FY202X is dated `202(X-1)-12-31`, not `202(X)-01-01`. A naive parser that buckets contexts by calendar-date proximity to "period start" must account for this — the "beginning of period" instant fact is dated the *previous* day relative to the duration period's start date, not the start date itself.
- **Comparative periods expected** (`con_ComparativeReportingPeriodMustBePresentDuration`/`...Instant`, IAS 1-based, severity `warning`): at least two distinct `endDate`/`instant` values are expected across the document — i.e. current + at least one comparative period should normally both be present and both tagged (Guidance 1.2.2 also states: *"issuers are reminded that Annex III.1 of the RTS on ESEF requires to mark up all numbers ... which means that all comparative figures included therein need to be marked up"*).
- **segment vs scenario** (Guidance 2.1.3): the XBRL 2.1 spec allows dimensional members in either `xbrli:segment` or `xbrli:scenario`; **ESMA mandates `xbrli:scenario`** for ESEF: *"Extension taxonomy MUST set xbrli:scenario as context element on definition arcs ... xbrli:segment container MUST NOT be used in contexts."* (rule `con_SegmentContainerMustNotBeUsedInXBRLContext`, checks `count(//xbrli:segment) eq 0`). **A parser should read dimension members from `xbrli:scenario`, not `xbrli:segment`**, for ESEF instances (this differs from many other jurisdictions' XBRL practice). Also, `xbrli:scenario` must contain *only* XBRL-Dimensions-defined content — no custom XML (`fac_...` style rule referenced but the manual states this as a MUST).
- **Typed dimensions**: Guidance 3.2.3 — ESMA "recommends not defining typed dimensions in the extension taxonomy" and rule `typedDimensionDefinitionInExtensionTaxonomy` flags them as a violation if present. So a parser can, in practice, expect **only explicit dimensions** (dimension member = a defined domain-member concept, not an arbitrary typed value) in ESEF extension content — though this is a "MUST NOT" recommendation for preparers rather than a hard technical impossibility, so defensive code should still handle a typed-dimension context without crashing.
- **Default members**: every dimension (core or extension) has a globally-defined default member which preparers *must not* override (Guidance 3.4.3: *"The extension taxonomy MUST not modify (prohibit and/or override) default members assigned to dimensions by the ESEF taxonomy"*). The default-member ELR is `[990000] Axis – Defaults` at role URI `https://www.esma.europa.eu/xbrl/role/cor/ifrs-dim_role-990000` in `esef_cor.xsd`. Practical meaning for a reader: **"no explicit member on axis X" ≡ "the default member of axis X"** — this is standard XBRL Dimensions semantics but is explicitly reinforced here, and matters directly for §1 above (`Consolidated [member]` as default of the consolidated/separate axis).
- **"No dimension" meaning generally**: per the glossary's "domain" entry, an unqualified fact "applies to the entire domain or to a default member of a domain set in the taxonomy" — i.e. non-dimensional tagging is not ambiguous, it resolves to the default member for every dimension applicable in that hypercube.
- **Dimensional validity mechanics** (Guidance 3.4.2, useful for understanding *why* facts are dimensionally qualified the way they are): ESEF core line items are, by default, **not** valid for dimensional use at all — they're linked in the core taxonomy to a hypercube with a *null* dimension for both segment and scenario. To be usable, each line item (core or extension) must be linked into at least one hypercube in the issuer's extension taxonomy — either a "real" dimensional hypercube, or the special placeholder hypercube `esef_cor:LineItemsNotDimensionallyQualified` at ELR `[999999] Line items not dimensionally qualified` (role `https://www.esma.europa.eu/xbrl/role/cor/esef_role-999999`) for facts that aren't meant to carry any dimension. This placeholder pattern is worth recognizing if the parser ever inspects the extension taxonomy's definition linkbase, but doesn't affect instance-document parsing directly.

## 3. Units, decimals, scale, sign, and the "target XBRL document"

- **Accuracy attribute**: *"The accuracy of numeric facts MUST be defined with the 'decimals' attribute rather than the 'precision' attribute"* (Guidance 2.2.1; rule `fac_NumericFactsMustNotUsePrecisionAttribute`). A parser should assume `@decimals` is present and `@precision` is not (or ignore `@precision` if seen — it would indicate a non-conformant filing).
- **Scale is independent of decimals** (Guidance 2.2.1, quoted verbatim): *"the scale factor used in iXBRL is separate from the XBRL 'accuracy' mechanism (expressed using 'decimals' or 'precision'). For example, the value '$12.34 million' is expressed in millions (a scale factor of '6'), but is accurate to the nearest $10,000 (which would be denoted by a decimals value of '-4')."* Practical parsing implication: `@scale` only affects how the *literal text* in the human-readable HTML is transformed into the underlying numeric value (i.e. it's an inline-XBRL presentation/transformation detail); once you have the resolved numeric fact value (e.g. from the "target XBRL document" / derived instance), scale is irrelevant — only `@decimals` remains, describing rounding/accuracy of that resolved value.
- **Percentages/ratios**: tagged in decimal notation via `ix:nonFraction` with unit `pure` and `@scale = -2` (Guidance 2.2.2) — e.g. "81%" is represented as the XBRL value `0.81`. A parser reading percentage-typed facts from the resolved instance should expect decimal fractions (0–1 range, or beyond for ratios >100%), not "81".
- **Sign / signage convention** (Guidance 1.6.1, "Use of positive and negative values"): this is a *reporting convention*, not a syntactic attribute described in these documents as `@sign` — the manual does **not** discuss the Inline XBRL `@sign` attribute mechanics by name; it only covers the semantic convention:
  - Assets → debit balance, reported positive.
  - Equity and liabilities → credit balance, reported positive.
  - Revenue/other income → credit balance, positive; costs/expenses → debit balance, positive (subtracted in calculation linkbase).
  - Cash inflows → debit, positive; cash outflows → credit, positive (both positive, direction is conveyed by balance/label, not sign).
  - Some monetary elements (e.g. "Net cash flows from (used in) operations") are deliberately defined **without** a balance attribute, since they can be net positive or negative; this is called out as acceptable case-by-case.
  - **Gap**: the exact Inline XBRL `@sign="-"` mechanism (used when the *displayed* text is positive but represents a negative underlying value, or vice versa) is standard Inline XBRL 1.1 but is **not walked through in these three documents** — a parser needs the Inline XBRL 1.1 spec itself (or just consume the already-resolved plain-XBRL instance values, where sign is already baked into the numeric value and `@sign` is irrelevant).
- **`balance` attribute** interacts with the calculation linkbase's weight attribute (glossary: *"a designation, if any, should be the natural or most expected balance of the element ... and thus indicates how calculation relationships involving the element may be assigned a weight attribute (-1 or +1)"*). For a reader that doesn't process the calculation linkbase, `balance` mainly tells you the *expected sign convention* per §1.6.1 above, not the literal sign of any particular fact.
- **Units generally** (Guidance 1.7.1): use standard units from the XBRL Unit Registry (e.g. `iso4217:EUR`); custom units discouraged, and units should *not* bake in a scale factor (e.g. "millions of EUR" as a custom unit) — scale should be expressed via the `@scale` attribute instead. So a parser can assume monetary units are plain ISO 4217 currency codes, with any "millions/thousands" display handled purely through `@scale` at markup time (again, irrelevant once reading the resolved instance).
- **Inline document vs. derived plain-XBRL instance ("target XBRL document")**: per the Glossary,
  - *"Inline XBRL document"* = one XHTML file combining human-readable content and embedded XBRL tags.
  - *"Inline XBRL document set"* = one or more such files that, together, carry enough metadata to be mechanically transformed into...
  - *"target XBRL document"* = *"The XBRL-valid XBRL instance document represented by metadata in the Inline XBRL document set."* This is the classic `xbrli:xbrl` file: root element `<xbrli:xbrl>`, with `<xbrli:context>`, `<xbrli:unit>`, and plain fact elements (no XHTML, no `ix:` namespace) — the output of applying the Inline XBRL 1.1 unwrapping/mapping rules to the ix-tagged HTML.
  - Guidance 2.5.3 confirms **only one target document is expected per ESEF filing** (no use of the inline `target` attribute, i.e. everything maps into a single default target document) unless a national jurisdiction explicitly requires otherwise. So a parser can assume: one iXBRL document set → one resulting instance document, with no need to disambiguate multiple `@target` groups, for a normal ESEF filing.
  - Practical takeaway: **it is entirely legitimate — and probably the path of least resistance — for a Node parser to unwrap/transform the inline XBRL into the equivalent plain XBRL instance representation (contexts/units/facts) first**, and then reason about it using ordinary XBRL semantics (context ref, unit ref, decimals, dimensions), rather than parsing `ix:` tags embedded in XHTML directly.

## 4. Extension taxonomy anchoring — what a reader can rely on

- **Anchoring is mandatory for Primary Financial Statements extensions, not for Notes.** Guidance 1.4.1: *"Annex IV of the RTS on ESEF sets out that extension taxonomy elements marking up the IFRS consolidated financial statements' statement of financial position, statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, statement of changes in equity and statement of cash flows have to be anchored to elements of the ESEF taxonomy, except for elements corresponding to subtotals."* And explicitly for Notes: *"the RTS on ESEF does not set an anchoring requirement for the Notes to the financial statements. Therefore, if issuers decide on a voluntary basis to create detailed tag extension elements to mark up their Notes, there is no obligation to anchor such extension elements."*
- **Two anchoring relationship types**, both via the *definition linkbase*, arcrole `http://www.esma.europa.eu/xbrl/esef/arcrole/wider-narrower` (Guidance 3.3.1):
  - Extension **narrower** than a core element → extension appears as *target* of the relationship.
  - Extension **wider** than a core element (a combination of several core elements) → extension appears as *source*, anchored to each combined core element (Guidance 1.4.2), *"except where these elements are reasonably deemed insignificant."*
- **No duplication rule**: *"Issuers should not create extension taxonomy elements duplicating the meaning and scope of any ESEF core taxonomy element (Annex IV. 4(a) ESEF RTS) because they decrease comparability."* (Guidance 1.4.1) — so, by design, an extension element should never simply be a renamed core element; if a filer's `Assets`/`Equity`/`Liabilities`/`ProfitLoss`/`Revenue` line exists, it should be the actual core `ifrs-full:*` concept, not an extension. This directly supports the assumption that **top-level totals (Assets, Equity, Liabilities, ProfitLoss, Revenue) are essentially always tagged with core `ifrs-full:` elements**, not extensions — extensions occur for narrower/company-specific breakdowns (sub-lines, disaggregations), which is exactly what the anchoring mechanism is there to reconcile back to.
- **Anchoring data-type discipline**: ESMA "is of the opinion" (not a hard MUST) that extensions should anchor to core elements of the *same or more restricted* data type (e.g. `monetaryItemType` extension → anchor only to `monetaryItemType` core elements, not `stringItemType`/`percentageItemType`).
- **Practical implication for a reader that "only knows ifrs-full"**: A reader that ignores extension concepts entirely and only reads facts tagged with `ifrs-full:*` (plus whatever ESEF `esef_cor:*` placeholder/dimension machinery) will:
  - Reliably capture the mandatory core totals (per the no-duplication rule above), **as long as** the filer didn't have to define an extension *combination* concept that happens to be the reported subtotal (Guidance 1.4.2's "issued capital and share premium" example) — in that specific case, the *only* tagged fact for that combined line is an **extension** element, and the core "Issued capital"/"Share premium" components might not be separately reported at all if the filer doesn't disaggregate them elsewhere. So "core totals are always tagged with core elements" is **not absolutely guaranteed** — it's true for lines matching a core taxonomy concept 1:1, but a filer whose presented subtotal doesn't correspond to any single core concept must use an extension for that subtotal.
  - Miss anything the filer chose to model as an extension line item that doesn't map onto an existing granular breakdown — but per the anchoring rule, the reader can still recover the correct *wider* aggregate by walking the extension taxonomy's definition linkbase anchoring relationships (wider-narrower arcrole) rather than needing to understand the extension element's specific meaning. This requires actually parsing the issuer's extension taxonomy `_def.xml` file, not just the instance document.
  - **Subtotals are explicitly exempted from the anchoring requirement** (*"except for elements corresponding to subtotals"*, Guidance 1.4.1) — so an extension subtotal element might have *no* anchoring relationship at all, meaning a reader cannot always mechanically recover its composition via anchoring; it may need to fall back to the calculation linkbase (summation-item relationships) to understand how such a subtotal decomposes.
- **Domain members / dimensions / hypercubes also get anchored**, using different arcroles than concepts (Guidance 3.3.1): domain members via `http://xbrl.org/int/dim/arcrole/domain-member`, dimensions via `http://xbrl.org/int/dim/arcrole/hypercube-dimension`, hypercubes via `http://xbrl.org/int/dim/arcrole/all` — *not* the wider-narrower arcrole (rule: `anchoringRelationshipsForDomainMembersDefinedUsingWiderNarrowerArcrole` / `...ForDimensionsDefinedUsingWiderNarrowerArcrole` flag misuse of wider-narrower for these).

## 5. Annex II mandatory element list

**Not covered by any of the three provided documents.** The Reporting Manual repeatedly *references* "Annex II" (of the RTS on ESEF Delegated Regulation) as the source of the mandatory tagging list and of the textBlockItemType elements for block tagging (Guidance 1.3.3, 1.9.1–1.9.3), and a footnote explains its history (*"Before the 2022 amendment to the ESEF RTS, Annex II of the ESEF RTS contained Table 1 and 2 with the list of mandatory elements of the core taxonomy. The 2022 amendment to the ESEF RTS, applicable to financial years beginning on after 2023 only includes one table with the full list of mandatory elements of the core taxonomy."*), but **the actual table — element names, labels, QNames (e.g. anything like `ifrs-full:NameOfReportingEntity`, LEI-related elements, `DateOfEndOfReportingPeriod`, etc.) — is not reproduced anywhere in these three files.** None of `NameOfReportingEntity`, `LEI`, `DateOfEndOfReportingPeriod`, or similar strings appear in `esef_reporting_manual_2025.txt`, `esef_taxonomy_2024_documentation.txt`, or `esef_validation_rules_2022.txt` (checked by direct grep).

To get the actual Annex II list you need the RTS on ESEF itself (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/815, as amended, Annex II) or the ESEF taxonomy's `esef_all-pre.xml` extended link role that the taxonomy doc mentions holds this: *"esef_all-pre.xml includes a section (extended link role) identifying the elements that must be used in tagging when corresponding data is present in the report"* (Taxonomy Doc, Table 1, `esef_all-pre.xml` row). That ELR, not these text documents, is the authoritative machine-readable source for the mandatory list — worth targeting directly if/when the actual taxonomy package is available, rather than hand-transcribing Annex II.

## 6. Duplicate facts, calculation consistency, and pitfalls for a naive parser

- **Duplicate fact taxonomy** (Guidance 2.2.4, referencing an XBRL International Working Group Note): four classes — *"Complete duplicates; Consistent duplicates (numeric only); Multi-language duplicates (string only); Inconsistent duplicates."*
  - RTS rule: *"issuers shall not use numeric taxonomy elements to mark up different values for a given context unless the difference is a result of rounding related to presentation of the same information with different scale in more than one place in the same annual financial report."* So **inconsistent duplicates are non-conformant** (`inconsistentDuplicateNumericFactInInlineXbrlDocument` is an error-level check), but **consistent duplicates (same concept+context, same value, different decimals/scale) are expected and legitimate** — e.g. the same total shown once in the primary statement and again, rounded differently, in a note. A parser must **de-duplicate on (concept, context, unit) and pick the fact with the higher `@decimals` precision** rather than assuming exactly one fact per (concept, context, unit) tuple, and must not silently sum duplicates.
  - `tech_duplicated_facts1` assertion (Validation rules, "Technical validations"): flags facts that match on everything except value — i.e. inconsistent duplicates, checked via `xfi:v-equal($v1,$v2)` over sequences of matching facts, with message *"Duplicated facts with different values have been reported for: {concept}, value:{v1} != {v2} please review selected entries."*
- **Dashes/empty cells tagged as zero, and asymmetric comparative tagging** (Guidance 2.2.5): dashes/blank cells representing "nil" in the human-readable report are typically tagged as the numeric value `0` (via the `ixt:fixed-zero` transform), *not* omitted — so a parser should not treat a `0` fact as "no data," and conversely, a genuinely omitted comparative figure (untagged) is a distinct, valid state (**except** in the statement of changes in equity, where the guidance calls out that the two periods "should have the same line items and a zero, if there is no comparative," vs. elsewhere line items legitimately differ year to year and an untagged comparative is acceptable).
- **Fact equivalence assertions** (Validation rules, "Fact equivalence validations" group, sourced from the IFRS Taxonomy formula linkbase 2021): a large number (dozens) of `equ_*` rules assert that the **same economic fact tagged two different ways must be numerically equal** — e.g. `equ_Continuing_and_discontinued_operations_CashFlowsFromUsedInFinancingActivitiesContinuingOperations` asserts that the non-dimensional concept *"Cash flows from (used in) financing activities, continuing operations"* must equal the dimensionally-qualified fact *"Cash flows from (used in) financing activities"* tagged with `Continuing and discontinued operations [axis]` = `Continuing operations [member]`. **This is a real pitfall for a naive parser**: the *same* number can legitimately appear twice in the instance — once as a dedicated non-dimensional concept, once as a general concept + dimension member — and a parser summing "all Assets-like facts" or building a flat list without dimension-awareness will double count. These pairs are only warnings (not hard errors) so both forms can coexist even when correctly filed.
- **Cross-period ("roll-forward") assertions** (`crossPeriod_*`, dozens of them, e.g. `crossPeriod_Assets`, `crossPeriod_Equity`, `crossPeriod_Goodwill`, `crossPeriod_PropertyPlantAndEquipment`, `crossPeriod_NumberOfSharesOutstanding`, ...): assert `beginningBalance + change = endingBalance` across an instant-duration boundary (e.g. opening Goodwill + movements during the year = closing Goodwill). Relevant to note-extraction/roll-forward logic (per the user's other notes-extraction work) since these define which "beginning/increase/decrease/ending" element groupings are expected to reconcile.
- **Axis/dimension aggregation assertions** (`dimAgg_*`, `dimAggPeriodAdj_*`, dozens of them, one per IFRS axis such as `Accounting estimates [axis]`, `Property, plant and equipment by class [axis]`, etc.): assert `parent (no dimension) = sum(children members)`. **Pitfall**: this means a parent total (e.g. `PropertyPlantAndEquipment` with no PPE-class dimension) and the sum of its dimensionally-broken-down children (e.g. `PropertyPlantAndEquipment` × `Land [member]` + × `Buildings [member]` + ...) both legitimately exist in the same instance and represent the *same* total two ways — again a double-count risk for naive flattening. Also relevant to the user's memory note about "de-duplicate flattened edges" work.
- **Positive/negative value assertions** (`positive_1`, `negative_1` groups, each listing a long explicit list of concepts, with numerous `<Explicit Dimension Filter>...[complement]` exclusions for cases like "Accumulated depreciation" members which are expected negative even though the base concept is normally positive): confirms empirically (not just as prose guidance) that ESEF expects specific concepts to carry a specific sign, with dimension-based exceptions. A parser doing sanity checks on sign shouldn't assume "all `PropertyPlantAndEquipment`-family facts are positive" — sub-components under axes like "Carrying amount, accumulated depreciation, amortisation and impairment and gross carrying amount [axis]" / member "Accumulated depreciation and amortisation [member]" are the *complement* case, i.e. expected negative (or at least not `ge 0`).
- **EPS consistency assertions** (`eps_*` group): assert `abs((profitLoss / averageShares) - eps) <= threshold` for basic/diluted EPS, both for total and for continuing/discontinued-operations breakdowns — i.e. reported EPS figures should be internally reconcilable from Profit/(loss) attributable to owners ÷ weighted average shares, within a filer-supplied numeric threshold (`eps_threshold` parameter). Useful as a cross-check if the parser surfaces EPS, but not something to assume always holds exactly (it's a `warning`, and a threshold-bounded check, precisely because rounding makes exact equality unreliable).
- **Components of equity dimension** (mentioned in the task, confirmed present): the manual's `[990000] Axis – Defaults` example (Guidance 3.4.3) explicitly shows `"Components of equity [axis]"` with default member `"Equity [member]"` — i.e. statement-of-changes-in-equity facts are dimensionally qualified by equity component, with un-dimensioned facts on that axis defaulting to total equity. Consistent with the presentation-linkbase roll-forward pattern documented in Guidance 3.4.8 (parent-child sum: e.g. `issued capital` under `equity attributable to owners of parent [member]` + `non-controlling interests [member]` sums to `issued capital` under `Equity [member]`).
- **Block-tagged notes granularity** (Guidance 1.9.1/1.9.2): the same accounting disclosure can be legitimately **multi-tagged** with several Annex II text-block elements of different granularity ("preparers should use each of them and multi tag the information to the extent that corresponds with the underlying accounting meaning") — so a parser extracting notes text by block tag must expect overlapping/nested text blocks tagging the same underlying HTML content, not a clean partition.
- **`ix:continuation`/`ix:exclude`** (Guidance 2.5.5, 1.9.3): a single logical block-tagged fact (e.g. one note) may be assembled from **multiple, non-contiguous pieces of the HTML document** via `ix:continuation` chains, or have interior text excluded via `ix:exclude`. A naive "read the DOM span for this fact's id" extraction will get an incomplete or wrong text block if it doesn't follow continuation chains.
- **`@escape` attribute rule** (Guidance 2.2.7): `textBlockItemType` facts always have `@escape="true"` (i.e. their content is escaped XHTML that must be parsed as embedded markup, not as literal text), while `stringItemType` facts should be `@escape="false"` unless they contain `<`/`&`. A parser extracting block-tag text needs to un-escape/parse the embedded XHTML for text blocks specifically.

## 7. Taxonomy versioning

- **Namespace pattern**: root URI for both folder path and XML namespace is `https://www.esma.europa.eu/taxonomy` followed by a version date `{date}` component in `YYYY-MM-DD` format (Taxonomy Doc §3.4.1). Entry-point/element namespace for ESEF-defined elements specifically: `https://www.esma.europa.eu/taxonomy/{date}/esef_cor`, canonical prefix `esef_cor` (§3.4.4). IFRS core elements keep the IFRS Foundation's own namespace (imported, not re-namespaced).
- **Two entry points** (§3.4.7): `esef_cor.xsd` (what issuers' extension schemas import — defines all taxonomy concepts, dimension defaults, and the "not dimensionally qualified" placeholder) and `esef_all.xsd` (full taxonomy content including all linkbases, for browsing/viewing).
- **A reader can determine the taxonomy version in use by inspecting which `esef_cor.xsd`/`esef_all.xsd` (or IFRS `full_ifrs-cor_20YY-MM-DD.xsd`) namespace/schema-location dates are imported** by the issuer's extension schema, or equivalently which namespace URIs appear on the facts/dimensions in the instance (the `{date}` component of the `esma.europa.eu/taxonomy/{date}/...` namespace, or the IFRS Foundation's own dated namespace on `ifrs-full:` elements).
- **Which taxonomy version applies to which financial year is not itself stated in these three documents** — Guidance 3.1.2 only says: *"The RTS on ESEF specify which taxonomy version preparers are allowed to apply for each reporting period"* — i.e. this mapping lives in the RTS itself (an EU legal instrument), not in the Reporting Manual or the 2024 Taxonomy Documentation. The one concrete, dated fact given is that the **2024 ESEF taxonomy applies DTR (Data Types Registry) version `2022-03-31`**, vs. an earlier ESEF 2022 taxonomy that used `2020-01-21` (Guidance 3.2.2, listing both, with the 2024 taxonomy explicitly using `https://www.xbrl.org/dtr/type/2022-03-31/types.xsd`).
- **IFRS Taxonomy relationship**: ESEF Taxonomy = FULL IFRS Taxonomy (not the "management commentary"/other-entry-point variants) directly imported (elements, labels, references) + ESMA's own limited extensions/customisations (Taxonomy Doc §3.3.1, §3.5.1: fewer files, "guidance elements," a mandatory-tagging ELR, an "anti-reporting" mechanism to prohibit reporting of any core element unless placed in an issuer extension, translated labels). Presentation/definition/calculation relationships, however, are **customised by ESMA**, not directly imported from the IFRS Taxonomy's own linkbases.
- **IFRS 18/19 transition note** (from the Introduction, dated October 2025 in this copy of the manual): the 2025-update ESEF taxonomy introduces **two separate entry points**, one specifically for IFRS 18 (*Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements*, mandatory from FY starting on/after 1 January 2027), ahead of that mandatory date, and IFRS 18/19 elements *"may only be used once these standards have been formally endorsed at EU level."* Relevant forward-looking detail: expect a taxonomy/entry-point fork around FY2027 filings.
- **Calculation specification version matters for calc-consistency checks**: from the IFRS Taxonomy 2024 (and ESEF taxonomies built on it) onward, **Calculations 1.1** applies (arcrole `https://www.xbrl.org/2023/arcrole/summation-item`), superseding the plain XBRL 2.1 `summation-item` arcrole used in older taxonomies (Guidance 3.4.1). A parser that validates calculation linkbase totals needs to know which arcrole/spec version the filing's taxonomy year uses, or it will misinterpret/mis-flag consistency.

## 8. Danish-specific practice

**Not mentioned anywhere in these three documents.** A direct search for "Danish", "Denmark", "Erhvervsstyrelsen", and "DK" across all three files returned zero hits. Any Danish-specific practice (e.g. Erhvervsstyrelsen's own technical rules, naming conventions, or additional validations for ESEF filings submitted via the Danish OAM) would need to come from a separate Erhvervsstyrelsen source — note that a related file `erst_tekniske_regler_2022.txt` exists alongside these in the same docs folder and covers the **Danish ÅRL/GSD taxonomy** (`gsd:NameOfReportingEntity`, etc.), which is a **different, non-IFRS taxonomy** from ESEF/IFRS-full — do not conflate the two when building IFRS-DK/ESEF support; `gsd:*` elements are not part of the ESEF/`ifrs-full:` universe covered by these three files.
